Dataset

A missing value is not a zero. Under zero a region claims to have nothing, so it scores 100% area error whatever it does and is shoved around by its neighbours; that is why max area error reads 100% on any dataset with gaps. drop removes it and says so.

Method

Shape versus accuracy: raising cutoff to ~20 and anti-blob to ~0.8 keeps coastlines straighter and regions less circular, but costs area accuracy and speed. Worth it for a dozen large regions, not for hundreds of small ones.

Each pass re-rasterizes the current map and halves the blur. Small regions need a finer grid: they are only as accurate as the cells they cover, and a region smaller than one cell can only be approximated — which is why auto is the default here. It sizes the grid to the smallest region that carries real value, and grid used below reports what it chose. On NUTS 3 that is 1024 rather than 256, and the difference is 4% area error against 16%: at a coarse grid you are reviewing the grid, not the method. It costs the time — 20 to 35 s on the larger sets against 2 to 7 s on the smaller ones. Pin a fixed grid when comparing runs, or for a fair race against go-cart, which is hard-wired to 512.

The reference C implementation, compiled to WebAssembly. It takes no parameters: its grid is fixed at 512 (#define L (512)), as are its 1% error target and 1.5 padding. For a fair speed comparison pin flow to grid 512 rather than leaving it on auto, which picks 1024 on most of these datasets and so does four times the grid work. Its runtime also varies far more with the data than ours: about 2 s on NUTS 2 but 25 s on NUTS 0.

Metrics

—

Area error is quadratic in what you see: 1% of area is 0.5% of length. A mean below ~2% is invisible — watch the max (one region plainly the wrong size), the rounding, and self-intersections instead.
